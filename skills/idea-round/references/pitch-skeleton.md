# Pitch skeleton

One story order serves the concept brief, the idea deck and the form essays. It is merged from four idea-stage decks (WasteOS, Fineprint, LINE MARSHAL, SURYA) and the proposals around them (MuleShot, FLOODLIGHT, ORCA). Each part below says what goes on it, the device that proves it, and lines that carried it.

## The spine in one breath

A dated incident with numbers, then "already solved in <year>" elsewhere, then a one-line reframe, the name and tagline, the verbs that do the work, a mock where the system admits doubt, why only this team can do it, metrics declared before building, impact with every number typed, the same engine at every scale, the hour plan and the pre-mortem, a small ask, and where every number came from.

A judge who reads only the headlines should still get this argument.

## Headline rules

- A headline is a claim, never a topic. "The Problem" is a label: put it in the kicker ("01 · THE PROBLEM") and make the headline say something.
- Two beats: a setup, then the turn. Short words, full stops.
- A number belongs in a headline when the number is the point.
- Every headline must survive being read with nothing under it.

## The 14 parts

### 1. Cover

- The product name, huge (LINE MARSHAL set it at 126 pt on A4; WasteOS at 200 pt with "OS" in the accent colour).
- A 3 to 5 word tagline: "Every line, called." "Ten agents. One safe decision." Longer lines (Fineprint's "We read it so you don't.") work as a cover tag above the name, not as the tagline.
- Meta cells: event, theme or problem-statement number, venue and date, team.
- A document serial and revision ("DOC LM-01 · REV 2.1"). It shows iteration and lets judges cite a page.
- A real photograph with an overlay drawn in the photo's own pixel coordinates and labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE OVERLAY" (LINE MARSHAL: corner brackets, the dashed limit line, "2.14 m TO LIMIT", a verdict chip). Without a usable photo, a product mock in live text labelled "CONCEPT UI · ILLUSTRATIVE" (Fineprint).
- Anonymous rounds: no team names, no logos, no contacts. The serial stays.

### 2. Problem

- One concrete, dated incident, not a category. LINE MARSHAL opened on the 2023 Austrian Grand Prix: 1,200+ potential violations reviewed by hand, about 5 hours to the final classification, 8 drivers penalised after the race.
- Exactly three numbers set large, each with its source in the footer.
- A failure device: a mono log in which the backlog grows ("QUEUED (BACKLOG: 231)"), a cascade (WASTE GENERATED → NO VISIBILITY → FIXED SCHEDULES → OVERFLOW → CONTAMINATION → LANDFILL), or a dated claim timeline ("ONE REAL CLAIM PATTERN · FY24", Fineprint).
- A closing line that names the absurdity: "The sport that measures everything in milliseconds officiates its boundaries by eye."
- For rare-event problems, name the metric trap here: "A model that calls everyone legitimate scores 99.1% accuracy and catches zero." (MuleShot)

### 3. Gap and why now

- The "solved elsewhere" row, with years: Tennis AUTOMATED · 2006, Football AUTOMATED · 2012, Motorsport STILL MANUAL · 2026 (LINE MARSHAL). Fineprint: paying SOLVED 2016, cabs 2013, parcels 2015, a ₹3 lakh health claim MANUAL 2026.
- The rails that just went live and make it possible now (Fineprint: the national claims exchange live, 90+ crore health IDs, the regulator's response clocks, and the sponsor "in this room").
- The seat nobody has taken: "Nobody built the fourth seat: the patient's."
- MuleShot's version: three forces, then a band naming "the unfilled gap".

### 4. Reframe and solution

- A one-line reframe that moves the problem to where the product works: "We don't have a waste problem. We have a visibility problem."
- The name and tagline, then 3 to 6 verbs, agents or layers mapped to the user's journey: PREDICT · DETECT · SORT · OPTIMIZE · RECOVER · TRACE (WasteOS); Decode · Predict · File · Track · Fight (Fineprint); LISTEN · EXPECT · COMPARE · WARN (FLOODLIGHT); Flag · Score · Prove (LINE MARSHAL).
- Show the user's sentence, not a feature name: Fineprint introduced each agent by the question a user asks it ("Kya cover hai?", "Reject kyun?").
- One principle line that answers the judge's first doubt (is it a chatbot, will it act without a human, does it make things up):
  - "Agents draft. Humans fire. That discipline is the product." (Fineprint)
  - "Every answer is a tool call against live data. No tool, no answer." (WasteOS)
  - "The system knows what it doesn't know." (LINE MARSHAL)
  - "The LLM only explains the decision; it never makes it." (ORCA)

### 5. Product mock

- Built in live text (HTML or native shapes), not a picture of a design tool, so it stays sharp, editable and searchable.
- The featured case is the uncertain one, handed to a human. LINE MARSHAL's console selected car 18 at confidence 0.58, "ROUTED TO HUMAN REVIEW"; Fineprint showed "the honesty test, when the policy is silent" at 0.31 with a human handoff. A mock that only shows confident wins reads as a demo reel.
- A target split as a bar, labelled as a target (LINE MARSHAL: 80% of laps auto-cleared, 15% flagged with evidence, 5% escalated for judgment).
- The label inside the frame: an "ILLUSTRATIVE" ribbon, and a legend when some rows depend on data you do not have yet (MuleShot's cockpit: filled markers for signals modelled on the provided data, hollow markers for those that need production feeds).
- One line that keeps the human in charge: "Stewards stay sovereign; they just stop scrubbing video."

### 6. How it works

- Layers with one verb each: Ingest · Perceive · Locate · Decide · Present ("Five layers, one call").
- A budget bar: latency per stage (33 + 40 + 5 + 2 ms, under 2 s end to end) or cost per decision.
- "Why it holds up": the three or four objections a practitioner raises, answered (occlusion, bad conditions, setup time, doubt).
- Planned components drawn dashed and labelled "(planned)". The architecture diagram is one of the six quality-bar items.

### 7. Unfair advantage

- What you have that others will not: a data factory (LINE MARSHAL used racing simulators as free, exact labels), live public rails, the event's own sponsors wired in, or a simulation on real data (WasteOS on a real OpenStreetMap street graph).
- Show the arithmetic: "17.3 M labeled wheel positions" from 60 Hz × 20 cars × 4 wheels × 3,600 s.
- Close with the contrast: "Everyone else: hand-labeling YouTube clips (slow, noisy, copyright-encumbered). Us: a dataset factory that runs while we sleep." Fineprint: "Everyone else demos a chatbot that talks about insurance. Fineprint is wired into the exchange claims run on."

### 8. Proof declared in advance

- 3 or 4 targets with thresholds, each with how and on what it will be measured. LINE MARSHAL: ≥95% recall on violations (held-out simulator laps), ≥90% precision, under 2 s median crossing-to-alert latency (live), 80%+ of laps auto-cleared. Fineprint: under 5 minutes from photo to filed claim, ≥95% right first time, 100% clause-cited on a 200-question eval with 50 traps, under 2 seconds per Hindi voice turn.
- "Declared now, measured on stage." Say in the speaker notes that judges can hold you to them.
- Named naive baselines in the chart, the target labelled as a target: Persistence 0.18, Climatology 0.31, "SURYA (target)" 0.82.
- Anything already measured in the proof step goes here as MEASURED, with its interval and Methods line. Targets cover only what is not built yet.

### 9. Impact

- Every number typed with its evidence class. WasteOS's "Impact, and how we know" ran three columns: PROTOTYPE · TARGET 90%+, SIMULATED −98%, PROJECTED ~18 t CO2 per 1,000 bins a year with the derivation on the slide.
- A "NOT YET COUNTED" band for the biggest lever you have not measured ("recyclables recovered at source instead of landfilled: the biggest lever, measured on the rig, not assumed").
- Money in units a judge feels ("An ignored pothole is a 6× invoice.") and outcomes in people, not dashboards (FLOODLIGHT: minutes of warning, stock lifted, crews dispatched with evidence).

### 10. Transfer and scale ladder

- The dual layer lands here: the same engine on the real-world problem. LINE MARSHAL moved from race control to stop lines, wrong-side driving and level crossings on cameras India already runs: "The cameras are already up. They just can't see the line yet."
- A ladder of 3 to 5 rungs: NOW · NEXT · THEN · LATER · VISION, or PROVE · EARN · SCALE.
- The invariance line: "Same six modules at every scale: add bins, not rewrites." "Same five agents, re-pointed · zero rebuilds per rung."
- The market size, with its source, or in words when citations are forbidden.

### 11. Business model, or why this sponsor wins

- Include it when the rubric scores business or the sponsor runs the event. Fineprint drew a flywheel (BUY → CLAIM → TRUST → GROW → SHARPEN) and a four-part model (Attach · Serve · Keep · Lend).
- A sponsor-alignment table: what the sponsor is solving for · how we deliver it · where you see it (SBI Saarthi).
- Skip pricing when the organiser co-owns the IP: MuleShot dropped seat pricing and used impact and deployment instead.
- WasteOS and SURYA left this part out and leaned on technical proof and brief coverage. Do not force it.

### 12. Execution

- An hour-by-hour plan for the build window. LINE MARSHAL's, condensed: H0–2 rig up · H2–12 twin tracks, integrated hourly · H12–16 fusion and freeze, metrics on held-out laps · H16–20 real-footage demo, polish, backup video · H20–24 deck and pitch, rehearsed three times, ending "Presenter sleeps."
- The demo as one chain of steps: "a sim feed runs on stage → a car crosses the line → Line Marshal flags it in under two seconds → the console shows the overlay replay → one click, verdict logged."
- A backup video recorded before the last hours.
- A pre-mortem of 3 or 4 risks with the mitigation already planned ("Risks we've already planned for"). FLOODLIGHT printed owners on each phase and a "de-risked by design" line that answers "what if X fails?" before it is asked.

### 13. Close

- The tagline as the headline.
- The roster with roles (no contacts when anonymity applies).
- A small, specific ask the judges can grant: "A table on Oct 3. NHCX sandbox credentials. Eight hours. We hand back the claims journey Paytm can ship." (Fineprint) "Give us the surprise challenge. Watch the crew absorb it in 12 hours." (ORCA)
- Sources, photo credits and the disclaimer: "All detection overlays on photographs are illustrative mock-ups of Line Marshal output, not live system results."

### 14. Appendix: every number, and where it came from

- Three blocks: sources with dates, the method (simulation or measurement: seeds, assumptions, scripts, what is not modelled), photo and map credits.
- Footer it as outside the timed pitch.
- The number-taxonomy reference in this skill gives the labels and the block layout.

## One body of content, three formats

| Format | Shape | From LINE MARSHAL |
|---|---|---|
| Concept brief | dense, standalone, reads without a presenter | 8 A4 pages, DOC LM-01 · REV 2.1 |
| Deck | the brief re-paced as one idea per slide, speaker notes on every slide | 12 slides, notes such as "Team intro: 10 seconds." |
| Form essays | plain text, the same claims compressed | three essays of 1,700 to 2,000 characters |

Write the spine once, render it three times, and run the drift check before every submission. When an extra slide is needed for pacing (LINE MARSHAL's deck added "A simple rule. An impossible job.": 20 cars × 71 laps × 10+ corners ≈ 14,000 corner passes), its number goes into the Numbers table like every other.

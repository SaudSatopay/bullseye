# Pitch structures

How to shape the live pitch to the slot and the jury, with the openers, middles and closers that held up in the builder's finals and shortlist rounds.

## By slot length

Pace is about 140 words a minute. A live demo spends a third or more of the slot on screen time, so script fewer words than the slot could hold.

| Slot | Words if every second is spoken | Script target with a live demo | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 min | 420 | 250 to 300 | hook 0:20 · what it is 0:15 · demo in three beats 1:50 · proof 0:25 · close 0:10 |
| 5 min | 700 | 450 to 550 | problem 0:45 · solution frame 0:40 · live demo 3:00 · architecture and honesty 0:35 · close inside 5:00 |
| about 4:30, one presenter | 630 | 600 to 630 | cold open 0:25 · what it is in one breath 0:25 · demo act 1 1:15 · act 2, THE PROOF 0:30 · act 3 0:35 · architecture in 60 s · close 0:20 |
| 10 min + Q&A | 1,400 | 1,000 to 1,150 | problem with numbers · the gap and why now · reframe and solution · the product, live, chapter by chapter · how it works · proof against baselines · impact and scale ladder · close and the ask |
| 20 min judging call | 2,800 | a plan, not a script | problem in one breath (2) · both surfaces end to end (7) · under the hood, live (7) · the point (2) · who did what (2) |

Where these come from: the 5-minute shape is Dhaal's budget at MUJ HackX 4.0; the 4:30 shape is FLOODLIGHT's Round 2 script; the 20-minute call is CrackWatch's top-25 judging call; the 10-minute order is the skeleton the builder's 12 to 17 slide decks followed. The 3-minute shape is the 5-minute one with the architecture reduced to one sentence.

> Field note (Dhaal · MUJ HackX 4.0): The 5-minute script gave the live demo 3:00 of the slot and spent 35 seconds on architecture, honesty and the close: "The AI narrates the verdict; it can never change it."

## By jury

| Jury | Lead with | Prove with | What loses them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founders and VCs | the product and the money: who buys, why now, the wedge | ROI with its basis on the slide, a buyer named, a ladder (interview, LOI, paid pilot) | metrics with no business consequence |
| Engineers | the mechanism | latency budgets, failure modes, the live trace, measured numbers | hand-waving; "technical hand-waving will be found out" |
| Bankers, regulators, government | governance: maker-checker, audit trail, a human at the risky step | what is never automated, what is logged, how it complements the incumbent | autonomy with money or safety, flashy GenAI |
| Sponsor judges | what their platform does in the product that nothing else could | a primitives table, "what we did not have to build", honest friction | a wrapper; the sponsor tech as decoration |
| College and mixed panels | the criteria in their own words | a narrow end-to-end demo that never breaks, one real wow, impact numbers | ten features demoed badly |
| Mentors in a challenge round | the brief they wrote | each acceptance check shown, with the page | scope creep beyond the brief |

> Field note (Trinetra · ET AI Hackathon 2.0): Innovation and Business carried half the weights and the final jury was founders and VCs ("not metric-graders"), so the demo's response beat put the avoided loss per incident and an expected annual ROI "even at a conservative 1-in-15-year event" on screen, with the basis of every figure.

## Openers

- **A dated number from a real event.** "On the 26th of July 2005, Mumbai got nine hundred and forty-four millimetres of rain in one day." Then narrow it: "a flood is not a city-sized event. It's a street-sized event."
- **The judge's own experience.** "Every one of you deleted a scam SMS this morning." A universal problem every judge has felt beats a statistic.
- **The analogy row with years.** Tennis 2006, football 2012, motorsport still manual in 2026: the gap names itself.
- **The one-line reframe.** "We don't have a waste problem. We have a visibility problem."
- **Founder fit in the first person**, when it is true: StorePilot's README opened with the builder's own retail business.

## The middle

- **Demo in acts.** Each act has one point. One act is the proof: the negative case, the quiet day, the legitimate input that stays quiet. "Zero false alarms is not a claim on a slide. We just demonstrated it."
- **Architecture in 60 seconds with four verbs** (LISTEN, EXPECT, COMPARE, WARN), one sentence each.
- **Say the split exactly:** what the model does and what deterministic code decides. "The AI narrates the verdict; it can never change it."
- **Hand the judge the mouse** when the product can take it: let them drag the boat into the no-go zone and watch the system object.
- **Booth or attract mode** for floor judging: an unattended loop of about a minute through the hero moments, with a mute toggle.
- **Slides support, the product speaks.** Switch to the live app early; a product slide stays up only if the app cannot.

## Closers

- **The judge's own phone or data:** "Open your inbox. Forward us anything." Keep a printed card with the first beat's input in case their inbox is clean.
- **Round over round:** "Round 1, we promised this. Round 2, it runs."
- **A small, specific ask:** "A table on Oct 3. NHCX sandbox credentials. Eight hours." Name what you want from this jury.
- **Invite the hardest question** about the core mechanism; it starts the Q&A on your strongest ground.
- End on the product's one-line promise and the live URL, then stop talking.

## The 90-second cut

Write it when you write the full script, not when the organiser announces that time is short. Keep: the hook, one demo act with its number, the proof act, the URL. Drop: the second and third acts, the architecture, the roadmap. Time it once aloud.

VIALWATCH
Every dose, kept cold. Even at 02:10.

A rural clinic keeps a month of vaccines in one fridge. When it fails at night, nobody knows until the morning log. VialWatch reads every fridge every minute, forecasts a failure 45 minutes before the vials warm, and calls the one nurse who can fix it. 01 / THE PROBLEM

The paper log sees one number.
The vials lived the whole night.

Rural clinics keep a month of vaccines in a single fridge and check it twice a day by hand. A compressor that fails at 02:10 warms the vials for seven hours before anyone looks. The fridge had the data every minute; nothing was listening.

02 · The product
03 / SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Five layers,
one phone call.

Everything that must work at 02:10 runs on a ₹3,000 gateway in the clinic: no Wi-Fi, no cloud and no model on the critical path. The model forecasts; deterministic rules decide who is called.

Works offline

The gateway forecasts and calls over a SIM. Venue Wi-Fi, cloud outages and power cuts (7 days of buffer) do not stop a call.

Rules decide

A wrong forecast can change a ranking, never skip a call: the 8 °C rule fires whatever the model says.

Cheap to copy

₹900 per fridge, ₹3,000 per clinic, open firmware and no per-seat licence; a district rolls out without procurement.

Why it holds up

The parts that must not fail are boring on purpose: a sensor, a SIM, a rule. The model makes the warning earlier; it is never the only thing between a vial and a phone call.

04 / TECHNICAL PROOF

We found the trap
before the judges could.

Fridge failures are rare: a model that never raises an alert is right 99.4% of the minutes. Accuracy is the trap; recall at a fixed false-alert budget is the measure. We scored every number below with the scripts in the repository.

04 · Technical proof
05 / FEASIBILITY & ROADMAP

Proven where it matters,
honest about the rest.

The hardest risk, catching failures early without drowning nurses in alerts, is retired on held-out data. What remains open is listed with the step that closes it.

Proven now
  • Recall 0.91 at 0.6 alerts / week on 30 held-out failure days.
  • Offline call path: gateway to SMS to voice, tested with the network off.
  • Leak audit done; labels from the compressor log, not the door.
  • Latency: 35 s median from reading to ringing phone.
Open risks
  • Few real failures: 30 days, wide interval; pilot data narrows it.
  • Sensor drift in the field: monthly calibration check in phase 2.
  • Nurse response rate: measured in the pilot, not assumed.
  • Registry access: needs the state's API key; manual import until then.
